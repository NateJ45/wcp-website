## Post 1 — "When is the preschool cutoff age in Ohio?"

**Slug:** `preschool-age-cutoff-ohio`
**Category:** Enrolling
**Excerpt:** Age cutoffs decide which class your child starts in. Here is how
ours work, in plain terms, so you can plan the right year.

Age cutoffs are one of the first questions parents ask us, and the answer is
simpler than it looks. At West Chester Preschool your child's age on September 30
sets which class they join for the year.

- **Twos:** age 2 by September 30.
- **Threes:** age 3 by September 30.
- **Pre-K (morning or afternoon):** age 4 by September 30.

Ohio does not set a single statewide preschool cutoff the way it does for
kindergarten, so private and cooperative preschools each pick their own. Ours
lines up with how children group naturally by age, and it gives every class a
consistent range so the day fits the children in it.

If your child's birthday lands close to the cutoff, reach out. We have placed
plenty of "right on the line" children in the class that fit them best, and a
short conversation usually settles it. You can see each class, its schedule, and
its age range on our [class pages](/classes/twos), and when you are ready we walk
you through the next steps on the [enroll page](/enroll).

The best way to know the right fit is to come see a morning. [Schedule a
tour](/virtual-tour#sec-pp-tour-form) and meet the teachers.

## Post 2 — "What preschool actually costs in West Chester"

**Slug:** `what-preschool-costs-west-chester`
**Category:** Tuition
**Excerpt:** Most preschools hide the price until you ask. Here is exactly what
ours costs, every fee named, so you can compare with real numbers.

Preschool pricing is hard to compare because most schools will not post it. We
post ours, because a family deciding where their child spends their mornings
deserves real numbers up front.

Tuition at West Chester Preschool runs from $70 a month for our Twos class to
$200 a month for Pre-K, depending on how many days a week the class meets. That
is a fraction of what a typical West Chester preschool charges, and it is not a
trick. We are a cooperative, which means parents help run the school, so the
money goes to the classroom instead of to overhead.

Here is what a family pays, with nothing hidden:

- **Monthly tuition:** $70 to $200, set by the class.
- **Registration fee:** a one-time fee at enrollment.
- **Student fee:** $45 to $50 a year, for classroom materials.

Every number, per class, lives on our [tuition page](/tuition), and the co-op
commitment that keeps it affordable is spelled out on the [enroll
page](/enroll). If a month ever gets tight, talk to us. We are parents too, and
we would rather keep a family than lose one over timing.

Want to see where the money actually goes? [Come tour the
classrooms](/virtual-tour#sec-pp-tour-form).

## Post 3 — "Co-op preschool, daycare, or traditional preschool: what is the difference?"

**Slug:** `co-op-preschool-vs-daycare`
**Category:** Learning
**Excerpt:** Co-op, daycare, traditional preschool: the words get used loosely.
Here is what each one actually means for your child and your week.

Parents tour a lot of places before they choose, and the labels blur together.
Here is a plain-language guide to the three you will hear most, and where a
cooperative like ours fits.

**Daycare** is built around full-day care so parents can work. The day is long,
the focus is supervision and routine, and staff turnover can be high. It solves a
real problem, but it is not built around a teaching year.

**Traditional preschool** runs shorter days on a school-year calendar with a lead
teacher and a curriculum. You drop off, the teacher leads the class, and you get
updates. It is focused on learning, and you are mostly on the outside of the room.

**A cooperative preschool** is a traditional preschool that families run
together. The teaching, the calendar, and the curriculum are all there, and on
top of that parents help in the classroom, hold a small job, and make decisions
as a group. You are in the room for the years that matter most, and the cost
stays low because the community does the work a big staff would.

None of these is the "right" one for every family. If you want a long care day,
a co-op is not it. If you want to be part of your child's first school and keep
the cost sane, it is worth a look. You can read how ours works day to day on the
[co-op life page](/co-op-life) and see the classes on the [class
pages](/classes/pre-k).

Curious whether it fits your family? [Schedule a
tour](/virtual-tour#sec-pp-tour-form) and see a co-op morning for yourself.
